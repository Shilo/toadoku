# Usability principles

Product philosophy: **simplicity-first design**. Use strong defaults, minimal necessary features and progressive disclosure so ordinary play just works. Apply when changing features, settings, navigation or player-facing copy.

- Make starting, resuming and solving work without setup. Preserve meaningful task choices, such as difficulty.
- Omit unnecessary features/settings instead of hiding them. Add a control only for a concrete player task that existing behavior or a good default cannot adequately support.
- Place controls by task, frequency and consequences. Keep frequent actions easy to reach. Put necessary infrequent options behind clear secondary access, preferably one additional step from their relevant area. Technical complexity alone does not justify hiding a tool; avoid deep menus and rigid tap quotas.
- Keep solving tools in gameplay, task-specific choices near their action, and general preferences in Settings. Do not add an Advanced layer to Notes, Undo, Erase or Hint.
- Follow applicable system preferences, including reduced motion; remember supported explicit choices. Keep language and accessibility options discoverable, not classified as expert features.
- Use concise, familiar labels, consistent across English, German and Spanish. Explain consequences where they matter; defer optional detail. Preserve meaning when shortening copy.
- Save automatically under existing contracts. Surface essential feedback and recovery when needed, even for rare failures. Preserve destructive-action confirmations and assistance/reward disclosures; avoid confirmations for routine reversible input.

For new features/settings or material interaction changes, briefly justify the task, need and placement in the existing plan or review. Validate affected journeys and label untested assumptions. Minor copy fixes need clarity, meaning and localization checks, not a separate decision record.

This guide alone authorizes no app changes. Future authorized simplification may reconsider existing features and placement, including More, while preserving correctness, accessibility and progress.
